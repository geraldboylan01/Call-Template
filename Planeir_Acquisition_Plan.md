# Planéir — Two-Engine Acquisition & Growth Plan

*Turning the lead model into a build-able plan. Prepared for Gerry, July 2026. Numbers are live in `Planeir_Model1_vs_Model2.xlsx` (tabs: Engine 1 — White-Label, Blended Plan).*

---

## The thesis in one paragraph

Build one product — an Irish-tax-aware AI planning chat with visual explainers — and distribute it two ways. **Engine 1 (white-label API for advisers)** is the easy, recurring, no-CAC revenue that funds the company: advisers embed your branded chat on their own site and pay a subscription because it turns their traffic into qualified, pre-filled leads. **Engine 2 (consumer education funnel)** is where the scale and enterprise value live: a Planéir-branded destination that acquires consumers cheaply off the auto-enrolment wave, educates them, and — when they *choose* — hands them to advisers as consented, high-intent referrals, plus affiliate revenue. Engine 1 pays the bills and seeds demand; Engine 2 is the venture outcome. They share one codebase, one tax engine, and one compounding dataset.

Both halves are precedented: **Planswell** runs Engine 1 today (free planning tool, completed-plan leads, ~$450+/mo, one adviser per lead); **SmartAsset** ran Engine 2 to unicorn scale. Nobody does either localised to Irish tax, timed to auto-enrolment, with your visual/trust edge.

---

## The shared product spine

Both engines run on the same asset you've largely built: the AI planning chat, the Irish-tax calculators (SFT, ARF, lump-sum, mortgage, protection), and the visual explainers. Build once, deploy twice. Every consumer conversation and every embedded adviser session improves the same qualification model — which is the moat. Treat the tax engine + qualification model as the crown jewels; the two front-ends are just distribution.

## Engine 1 — White-label API (the early revenue)

**What it is.** Advisers and brokers embed your branded planning chat on their website. A visitor gets a genuinely useful, Irish-accurate mini-plan; the adviser gets an SMS-verified, pre-qualified lead with a completed data profile (25–40 data points, the fact-find half-done). It's "your own SmartAsset chat, on your own site, in your own brand."

**Why they pay.** It converts website traffic they already have into booked leads, pre-fills the regulated fact-find (the admin saving from your original model, delivered as a by-product), and modernises their client experience. Planswell proves the willingness to pay at ~$450+/month.

**Pricing.** €300/firm/month + €500 setup (in the model). That's mid-way between adviser software (£75–200) and Planswell ($450) — defensible because ~40 leads/year at £65+ each dwarfs a €3,600 annual subscription. Keep leads exclusive (one adviser per lead); shared leads are what advisers complain about elsewhere.

**Who and how.** Target the ~1,250 Brokers Ireland member firms. Start with 5–10 design partners you can reach directly through your existing network and the Planéir relationships you already have — the warm-start you don't have on the consumer side. Then a Brokers Ireland channel/partnership and case studies (leads generated, cost per acquired client vs. Unbiased).

**Revenue (model).** €36k → €216k → €720k across Years 1–3. Modest in absolute terms, but it's contracted, recurring, sold to a known audience, and needs zero consumer marketing spend. Its job is to fund the build and de-risk the P&L — not to be the scale story.

**Strategic bonus.** Every white-label adviser becomes a pre-committed *buyer* for Engine 2's consumer leads later, and every embedded session feeds the qualification model. Engine 1 quietly solves Engine 2's hardest cold-start problem.

## Engine 2 — Consumer funnel (the scale value)

**The funnel.** Acquire a consumer → they complete the AI education chat (understand their pension / mortgage / retirement) → a share consent to a warm introduction to a vetted adviser (sold as a qualified lead) → plus affiliate/commission on products placed. Model assumptions: 15% of visitors complete a chat, 20% of those consent, €80 per consented lead.

**The one risk that matters: consumer CAC.** This is where consumer fintech dies, so the plan is built around acquiring consumers *cheaply and repeatably* — owned and earned channels first, paid only to amplify what already works. Your unfair advantage is that your edge (education content, visual explanation, a QFA's trust) is strongest exactly on this hardest problem, and the auto-enrolment wave is a once-in-a-generation tailwind.

**Channels, ranked cheapest-first:**

1. **Organic SEO on the auto-enrolment wave.** ~800,000 people were auto-enrolled from January 2026 and are now Googling "what is My Future Fund," "should I opt out," "how much pension do I need." Own those queries with genuinely useful content and a free explainer tool. This is the single biggest, cheapest, most time-sensitive channel — move now while the wave is fresh.
2. **B2B2C distribution (near-zero CAC).** Employers now have a *legal duty* to help employees understand auto-enrolment but no tool to do it. Package Planéir as the white-labelled "understand your pension" resource that employers, payroll/HR platforms, and benefits consultants hand to staff. The opt-out window (months 7–8) is a built-in high-intent moment. Borrowing an employer's audience is far cheaper than buying consumers one by one.
3. **Owned video & social.** Short-form visual explainers are your demonstrated strength and the QFA credibility is the trust unlock. Low cost, compounding, and it feeds the personal brand.
4. **PR / earned media.** Journalists want plain-English auto-enrolment explainers; you're the ideal source. Free reach.
5. **Referral / share-your-plan virality.** Built into the product.
6. **Paid (last).** Only to scale channels that already convert profitably — never the primary engine.

**Monetisation, in order of value:** (1) exclusive referral fees to advisers — the core; (2) affiliate/commission on pensions, protection, mortgages placed — potentially large, needs compliance design; (3) ads — a minor layer that only matters at large traffic. Do **not** build this as an ad business.

**Revenue (model).** €145k → €870k → €4.35M across Years 1–3, driven overwhelmingly by referrals (ads are ~17%).

## The flywheel — why two engines beat one

Engine 1 gives you a roster of advisers *paying for leads before you have consumer volume*, which removes the marketplace chicken-and-egg on the demand side. Engine 2 grows the consumer top-of-funnel and the proprietary dataset. More consumer conversations → better qualification → higher lead value and conversion → white-label becomes more valuable → more advisers → more lead demand → you can afford more consumer acquisition. One product, one dataset, two reinforcing distribution modes. That compounding data-and-network moat is what turns this from a lead broker into a defensible platform (the Credit Karma / NerdWallet shape).

## Phased roadmap

**Phase 0 — Prove the easy engine + seed the hard one (months 0–6).**
Ship the white-label embed to 5–10 design-partner firms and get renewals + a lead-quality case study. In parallel, launch the consumer content engine and a free "understand your auto-enrolment / My Future Fund" tool as the SEO hook, and instrument the full funnel (visitor → chat → consent → appointment). Fund via Enterprise Ireland HPSU + HBAN angels.

**Phase 1 — Prove cheap, repeatable consumer acquisition (months 6–18).**
Scale white-label across Brokers Ireland; grow consumer organic to the first tens of thousands of visitors; sign the first employer/payroll/provider distribution deal; begin selling consumer-consented leads to your existing white-label advisers. Target: organic is the majority of acquisition and blended CAC sits comfortably below lead value. Raise seed on that proof.

**Phase 2 — Scale and expand (months 18–36).**
Enter the UK with both engines (the auto-enrolment/NEST playbook and the same tax-localisation moat, one market at a time); build marketplace liquidity and white-label net revenue retention; push consumer to 1M+ visitors. Raise Series A on consumer growth and the data moat.

## Risks and how the plan handles them

**Consumer CAC (the big one).** Mitigated by an owned/earned-first channel strategy, the auto-enrolment tailwind, and B2B2C distribution that borrows employer audiences. If organic doesn't compound, the consumer thesis is wrong — so prove it *before* spending on paid or raising the seed.

**Marketplace cold-start.** Engine 1 pre-builds the adviser demand side; supply (advisers wanting leads) is the easy side to start with.

**Regulation.** Stay firmly on "guidance/education, not regulated advice," and monetise via *consented warm introductions*, never data sale — that consent is the compliance story and the GDPR shield. Design for financial-promotion and introducer rules from day one; your QFA status helps. Keep any AI strictly assistive with a human adviser accountable, in line with the FCA/EU-AI-Act direction.

**Lead quality / adviser churn.** SmartAsset's recurring criticism is lead quality; your AI pre-qualification and completed-plan handoff is the differentiator — keep leads exclusive and measure adviser ROI obsessively.

**Timing.** The auto-enrolment search wave is most valuable now; delay erodes the cheapest acquisition window you'll ever get.

**Solo/key-person.** Use the pre-seed to hire; the plan assumes you stop being the bottleneck.
