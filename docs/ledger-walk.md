# Ledger walk

`js/planning/plan_pack/ledger_walk.js` exports four pure asynchronous functions. They do no I/O, consult no clock, mutate no input and keep caches within one call. They accept a compiled plan with `source.normalizedPlanPack` (including a current-position compilation), or the profile bridge's assembly result. The same confirmed revision, explicit calculation date, household, planning inputs, chapter selection and full B1 options price every candidate. A current-position pack with incomplete goal facts returns ordinary refusals with paths; the walk supplies no answers.

```js
const ways = await goalWays(compiledPlan, { id: 'draft', changes: [] }, goalId);
const priced = await priceRoute(compiledPlan, ways.ways[0].route);
const moves = await balancingMoves(priced);
const walks = await suggestRoutes(compiledPlan);
```

`priceRoute` is the pricing seam for a future `price_route` wrapper, not an analyst tool. It calls `compilePlanPack(..., {resourceLedger: true})` with unchanged current and the exact draft. `balancingMoves` takes this self-contained priced result (also returned as `way.pricing` and `suggestion.pricing`), rather than a detached ledger without its source household. Every way contains its wire route, complete compilation, ledger, refusal paths, goal shortfall and whole-route outcome. An engine refusal produces no fabricated figures. When an unaffordable cash instruction prevents compilation, a separately labelled `fundingReference` retains the native quote from that same draft with its cash instructions removed; it is not the refused route's ledger.

The reserve comes first. Protection remains a separate priced contract; no Illness Benefit or premium assumption is inserted. Funding order comes directly from B1: loans/cards, dated costs soonest first, mortgage, retirement. `available` reports cash after the existing draft and reserve, earlier goal IDs, and annual surplus after their planned funding. Earlier property-capital commitments and expense deficits are disclosed separately from earned yearly money. A prefix can be funded while a later goal remains short: `meetsGoalOnTime` includes earlier goals, while `wholeRouteMeetsGoals` and the full ledger disclose the whole plan.

For a debt or dated cost, ways are yearly funding, the native whole-goal lump sum and an explicitly labelled half-cash illustration capped by spare cash. Mortgage higher repayments and setting the term to the goal's age are equivalent wire choices in the existing engine, and are marked as such. No invented second mortgage formula is used. A draft's earlier assignments survive; changing a goal's funding replaces only that goal's cash instruction. Whole walks commit cash in method order and reprice before the next goal.

Retirement ways use the existing pension engine and goals-and-resources relief search: recurring contributions, a one-off personal contribution today, a declared cash/recurring illustration, a later age and a lower target. The last two require stated flexibility. Cash is distributed in proportion to eligible named members' remaining relief room, with residual cents in source order. This is a disclosed funding illustration, not a tax-optimised allocation or a ranking of accounts. `money_now` searches the smallest total gross amount within this allocation rule that closes the native pension-pot gap; when no such amount is available, it shows the maximum priceable amount and the remaining gap.

The catalogue's `pensionContribution` now accepts `annualAmount`, `oneOffAmount`, or both, positive and sourced, once per person. Current, recurring and one-off personal contributions together must fit the pension engine's current relief limit. The gross one-off amount leaves protected cash once and enters pension assets once. `oneOffPersonalContribution` extends the native pension input and is applied in the calculation year only; recurring contributions retain the existing salary-rate convention. Native cash flow's `cashAmount` identifies the part already paid from cash, avoiding a second payroll deduction. Estimated relief affects that calendar year's surplus, not immediate cash or subsequent years. The balance sheet, cash flow, accumulation and contribution-cost schedules share those engine inputs. A retirement target still beyond the native relief ceiling stays short.

`balancingMoves` finds each smallest single change holding the rest of the committed draft fixed. Cash, spending cuts and personal contributions search cents; target reductions find the highest feasible amount; ages enumerate years; target dates enumerate the earliest permitted calendar-year boundary because dated-cost pricing uses year offsets. Property sizing reuses `sizeMajorMove`, including its highest replacement price across years and earliest sale year. Whole investment sales and full outgoing clearances remain discrete; no fractional sale/clearance contract is invented. Missing essentials, acquisition facts, goal flexibility or other engine inputs return their paths. Scoped goal searches include earlier commitments and disclose whether the whole route balances.

`suggestRoutes` returns up to five independently priced whole walks in method-family/source order, with no score or rank: cash first, cash kept, smallest ordinary moves, and major moves where goals remain short. Identical wire choices are merged with their family labels. The plan-pack limit stays current plus three alternatives: suggestions are separate candidates for the analyst to retain. Ordinary combinations compose source-order priceable sales, method-order cash, the ordinary spending endpoint, stated ordinary flexibility and discrete clearances. Feasible combinations remove redundant changes and minimise remaining settings with the others fixed. These are conditional minima within that explicit construction, not an exhaustive economic optimum over all possible combinations or pension allocations. Failure says exactly that; it never claims that every ordinary combination is impossible.

At most one major move is allowed unless the compiler itself establishes the exception. It removes major changes, retains the committed ordinary draft, then prices the strongest supported spending move, every permitted major retirement age, and each property move at every sale year no later than the first funding gap. Trade-down trials use the minimum centrally permitted purchase price. Missing facts or adapters prevent proof. If one single-major trial or the ordinary draft funds every goal, the extra major is refused. Otherwise the priced ledger records the settings, outcomes, refusals, assumption version and date in `majorException`. No caller waiver is accepted. Suggested two-major routes use supported distinct lever types and minimise their settings conditionally; a coupled spending cut is checked again after stopped repayments. Multi-property timelines still require a separate transaction adapter and are refused explicitly. The proof concerns the same committed ordinary draft, not every alternative ordinary draft.

`npm run check:ledger-walk` is in the consumer gate. It prints `scripts/fixtures/ledger-walk.expected.json`: the five original invented forms and explicitly invented supplemental review households, including debt order, pension cash, the trade-down-only case, ordinary penny boundaries and a two-major exception. Every returned way and balancing choice replays through `priceRoute` and the direct full compiler, comparing every native input, chapter case, ledger figure and refusal. Tests also check source immutability, A/B/A and B/A/B order, a changed calculation date without a clock, missing paths, relief caps and one-off payments/relief not repeating. `--quiet` prints only the check result; `--write-examples` refreshes the reviewable fictional report.

Existing B1 limitations remain explicit: this is the working-surplus ledger, not the later complete retirement-years run. Missing exact route adapters for goals-and-resources, college and net-retirement chapters, and future-cost variants unsupported by the existing screen, retain their ordinary refusals. Protection uses the existing separate chapter and facts; the walk adds no default or scenario contract. No form question, worker route, generation code, screen or prompt-pack file changes here.
