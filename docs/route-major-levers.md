# Major property levers

`tradeDownHome` and `sellProperty` extend the catalogue in `js/planning/plan_pack/lever_catalogue.js`. Both are major illustrations. Every setting uses the existing fact/default/bounded source contract. The one-major-per-route rule applies, with only the compiler-proved exception described in [the ledger walk](ledger-walk.md). They require the full B1 ledger and a cash-flow chapter. `profileToPlanPack(..., {routes})` uses that mode for current and alternatives alike.

`tradeDownHome` takes `newHomePrice` and `year`. One home, its household owners, value and linked mortgages must be established, with `homeUse: 'throughout'` confirming full PPR use. CGT is then zero. The existing loans are settled and the replacement bought outright; no new borrowing terms are invented. The cash-purchase equity helper lives in `home_trade_down_math.js` and shares the existing engine's exact equity arithmetic. The route earmarks the chosen purchase price from that equity. Insufficient equity is a refusal. Native amortisation gives the settlement balance and when repayments otherwise end. A sale today uses the stated opening balance before any new payment.

Buying costs and progressive stamp duty come from House Purchase's existing rules and calculator, including its second-hand survey, legal, valuation, moving and contingency allowances. Selling costs use the proposed central agent percentage and legal allowance. A mortgage break fee, additional marketing, BER, or unusual title costs are not priced; a real quote may change the result. Home and rental values are held at their stated current values even for future illustrations, with no growth forecast.

`sellProperty` takes `propertyId` and `year`. The property needs its actual owners, base cost, acquisition date, acquisition method and home-use history. Missing facts are refused at their profile path. Equal joint ownership must be confirmed before using the tax engine's joint disposal contract. Pre-April-1974 acquisitions require the tax engine's missing market-value-basis adapter; they are not assumed. Current property and investment sales enter one native tax batch; future property sales carry forward the route's own earlier investment-sale state. No alternative shares tax history with another. The engine retains indexation, PPR/2011–2014 relief, losses, exemption, dated rates and announced-law disclosures.

Rental Keep or Sell receives the resulting tax and native loan settlement, together with stated annual rent, running costs and incremental rental tax. `HouseholdProfile.properties[]` preserves `baseCost`, `acquisitionDate`, `acquiredBy`, `homeUse`, `jointEqualOwnership`, `rentAnnual`, `runningCostsAnnual`, `taxOnRentalProfitAnnual` and `rentalIncomeSourceIds`. Rent less its stated incremental tax must reconcile with those bound net bank receipts. Other per-person bank receipts must be known, so stopping rent does not silently switch salary to an estimate. Repayments and running costs must be included in regular spending. Zero rent and no bound income records explicitly describe an unlet second property; the existing rental arithmetic now accepts that zero.

Form mapping version 1.1 preserves the existing annual rent and costs answers on the property, with their exact form paths and confirmed revision. A rental income created for a known sole owner is bound to that same property. Unknown ownership remains unresolved; no bank receipt, tax, acquisition date or base cost is inferred. The form's running-cost answer explicitly excludes mortgage interest, which remains in loan repayments.

A current sale changes the copied profile's property value, linked debt, rental receipts, spending and cash account. A future sale leaves today's chapter inputs intact and records the later transaction in the ledger. Later sales occur at the start of the chosen calendar year; current-year sales occur at the confirmation/caller date. The B1 surplus model still applies: working income shares end at each person's retirement, while an already-retired person's share continues. Lost rental receipts are removed only while that owner's share is still included, avoiding a second removal after retirement. The full retirement-years run remains a later step.

`ledger.propertyMove` records the engine inputs/results, costs, tax, settlement balances, source and timing. `years[]` records `propertyCapitalReceived`, `propertyCapitalUsed` and `propertyCapitalRemaining`. Proceeds cannot fill an earlier gap. New property capital pays expense deficits first, then goal funding in B1's existing goal order; existing spare savings still require `useCash`. Capital is used only once and earns no invented return while unallocated. Current sale proceeds already used by explicit `useCash` or the protected reserve are deducted before these annual withdrawals. Committed current proceeds remain assets but are excluded from free liquidity. `endingCash` and `propertyCapitalCommitted` distinguish these future commitments from today's cash balance and also appear in current-position differences. A pension target still short at the relief ceiling is not declared funded merely because property capital exists; no pension lump-sum adapter is substituted.

`sizeMajorMove(pack, routeDraft, {type, propertyId?, year?})` in `major_move_search.js` returns `sized`, `none`, `not_needed` or `refused`. Every probe uses `compilePlanPack(..., {resourceLedger: true})` with unchanged current and the candidate. Success requires a compilable route, the protected reserve, a balanced ledger and every goal covered on time. Missing facts and missing adapters return ordinary refusals. It never changes a goal's flexibility or overwrites the caller's draft.

- A trade-down search finds the highest feasible replacement price to the cent across the bounded years, using the earliest year to break a price tie. A supplied year fixes that date. Stamp duty is monotone, and replacement price changes only the released capital in this outright-purchase contract, permitting bisection within each year.
- A property-sale search checks years in ascending order and selects the earliest feasible year; a supplied `year` tests only that year. It makes no monotonicity assumption about the tax or yearly cash flows.

Neither search probes a sale after the draft's first funding gap: a later transaction cannot repair an earlier gap. This is a timing proof, not an assumed trend in property values or tax.

Proposed settings for approval live at `planeir_assumptions.routes.propertyMoves`, and are listed with status `proposed` in the figures register: a 1.845% gross agent allowance, EUR 2,500 gross sale legal/outlay allowance, replacement prices from 50% to 95% of current value, and a twenty-year search horizon. The agent allowance derives from one published 1.5%-plus-VAT fee example, not a market average. The legal amount is an editorial allowance: the [CCPC explains that fees and outlays vary](https://www.ccpc.ie/manage-your-money/buying-a-home/selling-a-home/), so it does not substantiate one universal fee. Sources, check date, basis and approval status travel with the result.

`npm run check:route-levers` prints wholly fictional inputs, priced ledgers, current-position differences and refusal paths. It is in the consumer gate. The tests prove the highest-price boundary with independent algebra and adjacent-cent enumeration, and the earliest sale year with an independent month-by-month loan recurrence and yearly budget. They cover future proceeds, native progressive stamp duty, a shared disposal batch, explicit zero rent, missing facts, no solution and repeated/reordered/clock-independent runs. Nothing here changes intake questions, worker routes, generation, screens or the prompt pack.
